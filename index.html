Throughline Learning

The clearest path to success

Everything a neurodivergent student needs to succeed in post-secondary already exists. The psychoeducational assessments exist. The accommodations exist. The assistive technology exists, and it works. The federal and provincial funding to pay for it exists.

What does not exist is a continuous support system from when a high school student is making application decisions, through their post secondary experience, and on to their transition to a career. At Throughline Learning, we provide that support system.

01 — The Problem

Eight Barriers to Support and Success

A family moving a neurodivergent student from grade 11 through a successful first year and ultimately on to a career has to navigate eight separate barriers to success.

To reach their potential, a student has to get past all eight barriers. Throughline Learning supports them each step of the way.

In February 2026, Algonquin College eliminated seven faculty positions in their Centre for Accessible Learning and replaced them with support staff roles. According to OPSEU, the new roles require less education and less experience. The college told students the change would align the service “with the services provided at other Ontario colleges.”

What that does to the service, in the union’s words:

“The accessibility of having a CAL counsellor who is able to meet the student's needs in terms of very individualized, very specific accommodations... They'll move to a more prescribed list of available accommodations; really just a checklist.”

Tracy Henderson, President, OPSEU Local 415

What it does to a student:

“I was with a person for about a year or so, and then I got an email saying they aren't here anymore and that I'm going to be with someone new from now on. In my case, that's really nerve-racking because I have social anxiety which then meant it'd take a long time to get comfortable with someone again completely out of the blue.”

Elizabeth St-Charles, second-year student

The pattern is national. Wilkinson and Martens (2024) assessed 86 Canadian universities and found accessibility information inconsistent and incomplete, published on websites that themselves fail accessibility standards.

Shifting student demographics hide the size of the problem.

HEQCO has reported college accessibility registrations falling, from 16.3% in 2020 to 12.9% in 2022–23, but this is a result of international students being unlikely to register (1.2%) vs. domestic students (23.5%). During the 2020-23 period, international students made up roughly half of college enrolments. Since the federal government changed study permit rules and quotas in 2024, international student enrolments have fallen by as much as 75% at some institutions. Demand for accessibility services from domestic students continues to grow.

03 — The Venture

One integrated service, from high school to career

Throughline Learning is an online, two-person practice working with families from program selection, through the transition into post-secondary, and on to launching a career.

The Psychotherapist

I’m Anna Veronica, a registered psychotherapist and experienced accessibility counsellor. I interpret the psychoeducational assessment, work out what the student needs, explain it in terms everyone can understand, and support the student and their family through all the transitions from high school to post secondary to employment.

The Educational Technologist

I’m John, an educational technologist and instructional designer. I put together the technology setup to support Anna Veronica’s recommendations, teach the student to use it, and do periodic check-in sessions to make sure they stay on track.

Why assistive technology often fails

The tools exist and many of them are good. The problem is not a missing tool. It is that giving a student a tool does not mean they can use it effectively. The two leading predictors of technology abandonment are tools chosen without student input and tools provided without student buy-in.

We are not reinventing the wheel. We are teaching students how to drive.

What about the free programs?

Some institutions run their own transition programs. Toronto Metropolitan University's SHIFT is free to incoming students with documented disabilities, and students should use it. It begins after admission and accessibility registration, which is more than a year after Throughline Learning’s services start. Our students arrive at their college or university’s Accessibility Services knowing what they need and prepared to succeed.

04 — The Engagement

Twenty hours, timed for maximum value

Throughline Learning’s 20-hour Preparation and Transition package is $5,000, which is a 10% discount on our hourly rate of $275. The package runs from grade 11 course selection to the end of first year. Sessions are timed around the post secondary dates and deadlines that require informed decisions and actions.

Additional sessions are available at $275/hr. Clients can schedule additional sessions at any time during or after their Preparation and Transition package. Many clients continue to work with Throughline Learning through the rest of their post-secondary journey and transition to work.

The self-serve library is built to the standard it teaches: audio-first or genuinely multimodal rather than text with an optional audio button, short segments, WCAG and AODA compliant. Most free resources for neurodivergent students are walls of text delivered in a PDF. Throughline Learning’s resources meet students where they are.

The library content is evergreen material, covering topics like understanding your own cognitive profile, what accommodations are for, how to have the conversation with an instructor, and how to build a habit around a technology tool.

In addition to the public- and client-facing resources outlined above, we are building a database of per-institution documentation requirements, funding rules, institutional processes and deadlines. 08 — Why Us

The competency pairing is the product

A psychotherapist who can read an assessment and tell a family what it means about their child usually cannot build the technology setup around it. An educational technologist who can build that setup is not qualified to interpret the assessment.

Educational consultants, executive function coaches and neurodiversity-affirming therapists each serve part of this market. What is uncommon is the combination. Clinical interpretation, guidance on institution fit, and technology implementation are normally bought from three people.

Anna Veronica is a registered psychotherapist with 10+ years of experience as a post-secondary accessibility counsellor, as well as experience as a faculty member and associate dean. She has a unique depth of knowledge in how neurodivergence, documentation and accommodation interact with the multiple systems and processes of a post secondary institution.

John has 30 years of experience in the post secondary education system. In that time he has worked in admissions and recruitment, online education, instructional design, and professional development, as well as 15 years as part-time faculty. He has an unparalleled breadth of experience across the entire student life-cycle.
